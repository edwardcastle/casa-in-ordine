The cellar, garage and storage room are where everything we don't know what to do with ends up: tools, unopened moving boxes, Christmas decorations, sports gear, old paperwork. Over time they turn into a chaotic warehouse where you can't find anything and can barely walk. The good news: with a clear method, these spaces can become the most organized and functional in your home. Here's how to organize garage storage and cellars so they finally work.

## 1. Empty out and declutter before organizing

There's no point organizing clutter: first you have to reduce it. As much as possible, **take everything out** and empty the space. Only when you see years of accumulation laid out on the floor do you realize the duplicates, the broken items, and the things you forgot you owned.

As you empty, sort everything into three groups: **keep**, **donate or sell**, **throw away**. Be honest about [boxes never opened since the move](/en/blog/decluttering-prima-di-un-trasloco) and rusty tools: if you haven't looked for them in years, you probably don't need them. The less you keep, the easier everything else becomes.

## 2. Set zones by category

A storage space works when everything has its own area. Before you put anything back, assign each wall or shelf a **themed zone**:

- **Tools and DIY**: drill, toolbox, screws and hardware, paint.
- **Sports and leisure**: bikes, suitcases, tents, beach or mountain gear.
- **Seasonal**: holiday decorations, fans, off-season blankets and duvets.
- **Archive**: documents, keepsakes, boxes to keep but rarely use.

Keep what you use often near the entrance, and push to the back or up high what you open only once or twice a year.

## 3. Make the most of shelving and walls

In these spaces the floor is precious and should be freed up. The rule is to **go vertical**: sturdy floor-to-ceiling shelving multiplies capacity without taking up more footprint. Choose adjustable shelves with a good load rating to hold heavy crates and tools.

Use the walls too: **pegboards**, hooks and magnetic strips keep tools and bikes suspended and always in sight. Anything hanging frees up floor space and is found in an instant.

## 4. Closed, labeled bins

Open cardboard boxes are enemy number one: they sag with humidity, attract pests, and hide their contents. Replace them with **closed plastic bins** that are clear or at least uniform, and stack stably.

Label each bin legibly, noting the category and the main items inside. A clear label saves you from opening five boxes to find the tree lights. For small DIY parts, organizers with compartments or clear jars keep screws and bolts sorted and visible at a glance.

## 5. Protect against damp and pests

Cellars and garages are often cool and humid, conditions that ruin paper, fabric and wood. A few precautions make all the difference:

- **Lift everything off the floor** with pallets or platforms: never set boxes directly on the ground.
- Use **moisture absorbers** or a small dehumidifier if the space is especially damp.
- Store fabrics and clothing in **sealed containers** with natural moth-repellent sachets.
- Periodically check for mold, leaks, or signs of rodents and insects.

## 6. Keep a clear path and know what NOT to store

However full it gets, the space must stay **walkable**: always leave a clear aisle to reach every zone without moving ten boxes. Keep a small buffer area for new arrivals, so you don't end up piling everything by the entrance.

Finally, remember that cellars and garages **aren't right for everything**. Avoid storing:

- **Important documents** and precious photos, which humidity easily ruins.
- **Food and drinks** outside sealed containers, since they attract pests.
- **Flammable products** near heat sources or electrical systems.
- Delicate electronics that suffer from damp and temperature swings.
